## Problem Statement

Conversational AI (ChatGPT, Claude) is powerful but linear — it outputs text,
and the thinking stays invisible. When you're working through something complex
(designing a system, mapping trade-offs, exploring an architecture), text alone
forces you to hold a mental picture that keeps collapsing. The visual layer
disappears before it crystallizes.

Current workaround: people give up on the visual layer entirely. They accept
text-only thinking and lose the threads they were trying to hold.

This product proposes: what if the AI drew while it thought? Real-time voice
conversation where a canvas materializes alongside the dialogue — a thinking
partner that makes the reasoning visible.

---

## Demand Evidence

**What exists:** Personal use — the founder built this for a problem they personally
have. Three specific cognitive use cases identified:
1. Mapping complex systems where relationships are hard to hold in text
2. Brainstorming where spatial arrangement matters (grouping, clustering, proximity)
3. Explaining something to yourself — drawing it out to realize what you actually believe

**What's missing:** No external validation yet. Zero non-founder users. No one
has been shown this product and said "I need this."

**Key insight on status quo:** When asked what people do right now to get this,
the answer was "nothing — they give up on the visual layer." This is ambiguous:
it could mean unmet demand with no solution (opportunity), or it could mean people
don't feel the pain strongly enough to change behavior (no market). This is the
central unknown to resolve.

---

## Status Quo

No tool exists that combines real-time voice + AI + visual canvas seamlessly.
The closest approximations:
- ChatGPT Canvas / Claude — text with some formatting, no real spatial canvas
- Napkin.ai — text-to-diagram but not voice, not conversational
- Miro + ChatGPT — two separate tools, copy-paste friction, no sync
- Excalidraw + LLM — engineer draws manually, no AI co-drawing

The cobbled-together workflow (if it exists at all): switch between a chat
window and a whiteboard, manually redraw things the AI described in text.
Most people don't do this. They just read.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Current framing:** The product is framed as an education tool for CBSE/JEE students.
This is the wrong wedge for the actual product vision.

**Better target:** An engineer or architect thinking through a complex system —
someone who needs to understand how pieces connect, where failure modes live,
what to build first. They're already comfortable with AI; they just wish it
could draw while it thinks.

**Important nuance on control (surfaced during this session):** Engineers who
think visually often want to control the drawing themselves. They don't want
an AI imposing a layout — they want to sketch rough shapes and have the AI
fill in, label, extend, or suggest what's missing. This points toward a
**collaborative canvas** model: user draws/moves, AI co-pilots. Not "AI
takes the wheel."

**Narrowest wedge:** One specific workflow — "I'm designing a system from scratch
and want to think out loud while something draws the architecture." A single
30-minute session use case.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Ship Narrow, Find Truth Fast (Recommended)
Fix the three P0 bugs (context window, SQLite WAL, copy rebrand), then put
the product in front of 10 engineers this week. Watch them use it. The goal
is not adoption — it's to find the one moment where the visual + voice combination
creates a genuinely different experience than text-only AI.

Effort: S — ~1 day of fixes, ~1 week of user conversations
Risk: Low — worst case you learn what's wrong faster
Pros: Real feedback beats all planning; product is already built
Cons: P0 bugs may kill the magic before users see it

### Approach B: Build the Killer Demo First
Before showing anyone, identify the single most visually impressive workflow
the product can demonstrate — e.g., "engineer describes a microservices system
and the canvas draws it live, component by component." Polish that end-to-end.
Record it. Use it to find users and signal to potential backers.

Effort: M — 1 week to build the demo scenario
Risk: Medium — might polish the wrong thing
Pros: Demo is a forcing function; engineers share impressive tools
Cons: Still no real user feedback; demo magic often doesn't survive contact with real work

### Approach C: Validate the Voice Premise First
Test whether voice is actually the magic or text+canvas works equally well
for engineers. Run a 1-week experiment with text-first mode and 3 engineers.
If voice isn't missed, it's a power feature. If it is missed, you have your answer.

Effort: M — 2-3 days to build text-first mode
Risk: Medium — may invalidate a core premise
Pros: The control question gets answered; lower friction onboarding
Cons: Dilutes product identity while testing

## Recommended Approach

**Approach A — Ship narrow, find truth fast.**

But with one modification from the session: when watching those 10 engineers,
pay specific attention to the control dynamic. Do they want to interrupt the AI
drawing and move things? Do they feel like the canvas is happening *to* them or
*with* them? That observation will tell you whether to double down on AI-driven
canvas or evolve toward the collaborative model.

The rebrand is not a rebuild. It's a landing page headline, a few product copy
changes, and removing education-specific agent prompts. One day of work.

---

## Open Questions

1. **The control dynamic**: Is AI-driven canvas actually what engineers want, or
   do they want to co-draw? This needs to be observed in real sessions, not assumed.

2. **The status quo ambiguity**: "People give up on the visual layer" — is this
   unmet demand waiting to be unlocked, or evidence that the pain isn't strong enough
   to change behavior? The answer comes from watching 10 people use it.

3. **Voice vs. text for the first session**: Should new users start with voice or
   text? Voice has more magic but higher friction (mic permissions, talking out loud
   in an office/library). This matters for the first impression.

4. **The right vertical**: Engineers designing systems are one answer. Others:
   founders mapping their business model, consultants structuring a client problem,
   researchers building a mental model. Which has the most concentrated pain?

## Success Criteria

After this phase, these should be true:
1. At least 1 non-founder person has used the product in their real workflow

2. That person described a specific moment where the visual + voice changed their

3. They asked to use it again without being prompted
4. The control dynamic question has a concrete answer based on observation

## The Assignment

**This week:** Find one engineer — a friend, a classmate, someone you respect —
who is currently working through a complex system design problem. Not hypothetical.
Currently working. Sit next to them (or share screen), hand them the product,
say "try to think through your problem using this," and say nothing else.
Watch what they do. Don't explain, don't help, don't rescue.

That one session will tell you more than three more months of building.
