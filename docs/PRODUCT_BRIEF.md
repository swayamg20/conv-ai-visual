# Murmur — Product Brief

## Overview

Murmur is a voice-first AI tutor that teaches by drawing.

Instead of answering with a wall of text, Murmur listens to a student in real time, reasons about the question, and responds with both voice and synchronized visuals on a shared canvas. The goal is to make difficult concepts easier to understand by turning explanation into something the student can hear, see, and follow step by step.

Today, the product is positioned education-first, with an initial focus on concept-heavy learning and exam-prep use cases such as physics and math.

---

## Vision

The long-term vision behind Murmur is that AI should not just give answers; it should make its thinking visible.

Most AI learning products are still text-first. They respond, but the reasoning disappears as soon as the answer is read. Murmur takes a different view: for many hard subjects, understanding comes from seeing relationships, setups, diagrams, equations, and intermediate steps unfold in front of you.

Murmur is built around the idea that voice is the interface, but the canvas is the workspace. A student should be able to speak naturally, ask follow-up questions, interrupt, and explore a concept while the tutor draws and explains in real time, like a strong teacher at a board.

The broader bet is that the next major AI interface is not just better chat. It is multimodal reasoning that can talk, listen, draw, and adapt in one continuous interaction loop.

---

## Product Idea

At its core, Murmur is an interactive visual tutoring session powered by AI.

The experience works like this:

1. A student speaks a question naturally.
2. Murmur transcribes the question in real time.
3. The system decides how to explain it.
4. As it speaks back, it draws diagrams, equations, plots, and step-by-step visuals on a shared canvas.
5. The student can interrupt, ask a follow-up, and keep the session going conversationally.

This makes Murmur especially useful for topics that are spatial, procedural, or hard to hold in working memory, such as:

- physics problem solving
- mathematical relationships and derivations
- diagram-based reasoning
- multi-step conceptual explanations

The product is not meant to feel like “ChatGPT with a mic.” It is meant to feel like a tutor thinking out loud on a live board.

### Key Product Characteristics

- Real-time voice interaction with interruption support
- Visual explanations synchronized to speech
- Education-oriented diagram and equation rendering
- Personalized tutoring through session memory and student context
- The ability to use uploaded study materials to ground explanations
- A live learning experience that is more active than static videos and more visual than chat

---

## Why It Matters

A large part of learning, especially in subjects like physics and math, is not just about receiving an answer. It is about building a mental model.

Students often struggle because most current tools explain linearly while the student is trying to understand something spatial or multi-step. A coaching video may be clear but not interactive. A generic chatbot may be responsive but not visual. A whiteboard app may be flexible but requires the student to do the translation work manually.

Murmur tries to close that gap by combining:

- the immediacy of conversation
- the clarity of a drawn explanation
- the adaptability of an AI tutor

The result is a product that aims to improve understanding, not just answer generation.

---

## Market Context

Murmur is entering a market where students already use multiple fragmented learning tools:

- coaching classes and private tutors
- YouTube and recorded lectures
- problem books and notes
- generic AI chat tools

Each of these solves only part of the problem.

- Human tutors are trusted but expensive and limited by availability.
- Recorded content is useful but passive.
- Generic AI tools are fast but usually text-heavy and weak at synchronized visual teaching.
- Whiteboard-style workflows are powerful but fragmented and manual.

Murmur sits in the gap between these categories. It combines on-demand AI tutoring with a visual teaching surface, which is especially relevant for concept-heavy subjects where students need to see how an explanation is constructed.

### Initial Target Users

The strongest initial wedge is high-school students preparing for rigorous, exam-driven learning tracks, especially:

- JEE / NEET / CBSE-style science and math students
- students who regularly get stuck on concept-heavy physics problems
- students who already consume digital learning content but still need interactive explanation

Secondary users may include:

- parents looking for a more engaging support tool
- tutors who want a visual AI assistant
- coaching ecosystems that want a more interactive practice layer

---

## Why Now

This product is newly viable because the stack has finally caught up:

- speech-to-text is good enough for fast, natural input
- text-to-speech is good enough to feel conversational
- model quality is strong enough to explain and adapt in real time
- modern browser voice infrastructure makes shared voice + canvas experiences feasible

Students are also already comfortable learning with AI. What is still missing is a product that feels more like guided explanation and less like a text box.

That is the opening Murmur is pursuing.

---

## Differentiation

Murmur’s differentiation is not just “we use AI for tutoring.” Many products can say that.

The real differentiation is the integrated experience:

- voice-first interaction
- real-time visual reasoning
- synchronized explanation and drawing
- an education-oriented visual component library
- memory across sessions
- support for grounding explanations in student materials

The moat is not only the model layer. It is the combination of low-latency voice, structured scene generation, tutoring-specific visuals, and a product interaction model built for understanding rather than text response alone.

## GTM Strategy

The near-term GTM should stay narrow and evidence-driven.

### Initial Wedge

Start with a single strong use case:

“I am stuck on a physics concept or problem, and I want an AI tutor that explains it live with voice and diagrams.”

This is a better wedge than trying to launch as a broad “AI tutor for everything.”

### Early GTM Motion

- Run a small closed beta with 10-20 education-focused users
- Prioritize students preparing for concept-heavy exams
- Recruit through founder network, classmates, tutor contacts, and coaching communities
- Watch real tutoring sessions rather than relying on polished demos
- Position Murmur as a visual AI tutor, not a generic chatbot

### What To Validate Early

The first question is not “Will everyone use this?”

The first question is: does this materially help the right student learn better than the current alternatives?

The strongest early validation signals are:

- students come back without being prompted
- they use it for real homework or exam prep
- they report that a confusing topic became clearer
- they prefer it to rewatching a lecture or reading a static solution
- they use the visual explanation loop repeatedly, not just once for novelty

Useful early product metrics include:

- repeat usage within a week
- session length
- number of completed problem-solving sessions
- usage of uploaded study materials
- qualitative feedback on clarity and confidence

---

## Near-Term Product Focus

For the current branch and product direction, the focus should remain on polishing the education experience before broadening the market story.

That means improving:

- session reliability
- memory across interactions
- physics-specific visual teaching quality
- use of uploaded learning resources
- overall student trust in the tutoring flow

Positioning experiments outside education can be explored later in a separate branch, but the current product story should stay consistent: Murmur is building a visual, voice-first AI tutor for difficult concepts.
